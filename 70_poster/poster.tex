\section{Motivation}

Oil slicks ... Sentinel-1 SAR imagery ...

Our research questions (RQs) are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} How well can deep learning detect oil slicks in Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, and how does performance change between an in-distribution random split and a geographic out-of-distribution split (Mediterranean)?
  \item \textbf{RQ2:} Can a Geospatial Foundation Model improve over a CNN baseline, and what do XAI methods reveal about the features that drive the predictions?
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}
Our methodology is structured as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CNN baseline} trained from scratch on OilSlick
  \item \textbf{Terramind} \cite{Jakubik_2025_ICCV} trained while freezing the backbone and only training a classification head on OilSlick
  \item \textbf{Grid search} performed for both models (36 training runs)
  \item \textbf{Find best model} (based on val loss) for each model and split type (4 total) for individual final test set evaluation.
\end{itemize}

Before training, data analysis was performed, to understand the data distribution and apply imagery filtering.

\myfigure{0}{map.png}{0.95}{}{Geographic map depicting the global areas of captured positive and negative oil slick samples. Positive: red (ships), yellow (platforms), and green (natural seeps).  Negative: blue.}

Data filtering was crucial for this project, by finding corrupt TIFF files and imagery with >= 40\% of nodata in it.

\myfigure{0}{filtering.png}{0.95}{}{Filtering of OilSlick's Sentinel-1 data subset. The blue bars represent the number of images before filtering, while the orange bars represent the number of images after filtering.}
