\section{Overview}

\subsection{Purpose \& Problem Definition}
\subsubsection{What is the main objective of the study (prediction, classification, explanation, etc.)?} Classification, focusing on binary detection of oil slicks in oceans using Sentinel-1 synthetic aperature radar (SAR) imagery.
\subsubsection{What are the research questions or hypotheses addressed?} How well can deep learning detect oil slicks in Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, and how does performance change between an in-distribution random split and a geographic out-of-distribution split (Mediterranean)? An optional focus is whether a Geospatial Foundation Model (GFM) can improve over a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) baseline, and what Explainable AI (XAI) methods reveal about the features driving predictions.
\subsubsection{Why is ML an appropriate approach here (what alternative / statistical baselines would miss)?} Oil spills cause severe ecological damage. Furthermore, it is expensive to monitor the oceans manually. Automation through ML is essential for scalable detection and monitoring.
\subsubsection{What is the model used for (e.g., description, explanation, prediction/mapping, identifying patterns) and what assumptions guide this choice?} Primarily for prediction (classification of oil slick presence or absence). Since SAR imagery works at night and through clouds, it is suitable for the desired task. Moreover, oil films dampen short surface waves, visible as dark patches in SAR backscatter.

\subsection{Phenomenon \& Targets}
\subsubsection{If applicable, what is the target variable (definition, unit, categories)?} An image-based binary classification label, indicating the presence (1) or absence (0) of an oil slick in a given Sentinel-1 SAR image chip.
\subsubsection{What is the temporal and spatial scope?} The spatial scope includes the Mediterranean Sea as an out-of-distribution test set in the geographic split. The temporal scope is not explicitly defined, but the dataset includes SAR imagery from 2015 to 2020.
\subsubsection{What assumptions or known constraints shape the study (e.g. stationarity, sampling limits)?} SAR imagery is robust to weather and lighting conditions. The key challenge is distinguishing oil slicks from ''lookalikes'', such as natural slicks, low-wind areas, or ship wakes.

\subsubsection{What are the data types (e.g., images, text, measurements, etc.) and their sources (e.g., personal datasets, excavation records, etc.)?} The source of the data is the OilSlick subset of the WaterBench dataset, and it consists of images and tabular metadata. The imagery is based on 2-band GeoTIFFs from Sentinel-1 SAR, with VV and VH backscatter channels. Metadata is provided in CSV and JSON formats.

\subsection{Splitting (anti-leakage)}
\subsubsection{How were the training, validation, and test splits defined (randomly, by time, by region, by group)?} Two splits are defined: (1) a random split for in-distribution data, and (2) a geographic split with the Mediterranean as an out-of-distribution set.
\subsubsection{How was overlap or ``leakage'' between these sets avoided?} By using the \texttt{splits/random/} and \texttt{splits/geographic/} folders, containing pre-defined split TXT files to avoid any overlap or leakage.
\subsubsection{What share of the data went into each set (e.g., 80\% train set, 20\% test set)?} The random split is structured as follows: 900 Train, 150 Validation, 300 Test data. The geographic split is structured slightly differently: 864 Train, 217 Validation, 150 Test.
\subsection{Preprocessing \& Features}
\subsubsection{What preprocessing steps were applied (encoding, scaling, normalization, imputation), and where were they fitted (i.e., before or after the dataset splitting)?} Each Sentinel-1 SAR image chip is normalized by using z-score on VV and VH backscatter channels in dB. This is done after splitting to prevent data leakage. Each channel is scaled to 224x224 pixels, and the two channels are stacked to create a 2-channel input tensor for the models.

\section{Execution}

\subsection{Model Choice}
\subsubsection{Which candidate algorithms or model families were considered, and why?} On the one hand, a standard image-classification CNN (e.g., ResNet-18 or ResNet-50) is adapted for 2-channel input as a baseline. On the other hand, TerraMind, a pre-trained GFM, is considered to assess improvements.
\subsubsection{Which final model(s) were selected, and on what grounds (performance, interpretability, resource efficiency)?} As the baseline, the ResNet-50 CNN is selected for its established performance in image classification, using the pre-trained \texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V2} weights in \texttt{ResNet50\_Weights}. According to non-baseline models, TerraMind as \texttt{terramind\_v1\_small} is selected to discover if the GFM's pre-training on geospatial data boosts performance for this specific task, using its \texttt{S1GRD} modality.
